/ 010 — RULES THE REAL WORLD TAUGHT US

Group insurance is mostly exceptions. We model them.

Years of running endorsements on spreadsheets leave scars, and each scar is a rule. These are the ones the platform enforces today, each configurable per policy instead of buried in code.

Pricing

Pro-rata, per relation

Daily or monthly, set for each relation on the policy, so a spouse added in month seven is not priced like a child added in month two. Removals can refund the unused days, in full, or not at all.

joins on day 92 of 365 → pays 274/365 of the premium
Eligibility

Family construct

Which relations are allowed, how many children and parents, age limits, and the age gap a child must have from the employee or the spouse. Twins are allowed only where the policy says so.

child 26 y, limit 25 → line rejected, others pass
Who pays

Employer and employee share

Premium is split by percentage for each relation group: self, spouse, children, parents, parents-in-law. Pro-rata applies to both parts, so the two always add up to the whole.

self 100% employer · parents 0% employer
Money

Rounding, once

None, floor, ceiling or nearest, to a unit the policy chooses (₹10 by default). Applied in one place, after pro-rata and before tax, so every report agrees to the paisa.

₹1,234.56 · ceil to ₹10 → ₹1,240.00
Deposits

Shared deposit accounts

A client deposit can fund one policy, a group of policies, every policy of a company, or an arbitrary set. HR requests a top-up; the broker approves it; the ledger records both.

POLICY · GROUP · COMPANY · MULTIPLE
Structure

Parent and child companies

A holding company's HR sees its subsidiaries and only those. The hierarchy is one level deep and can never loop, and a parent cannot be closed while it still has active children.

head office → 3 subsidiaries · no grandchildren
Joining late

Mid-term joiners

Cover starts from the date of joining, from the day the file was uploaded, or not at all. Dependants have their own rules: a spouse added within 30 days of the marriage is treated differently from one added a year later.

spouse · 30 days · from the life event
Timing

Enrollment windows

A policy-wide window, a custom window for a single employee (it can extend the window, never shorten it), a hard close date, and a freeze on the day an employee confirms. Try it below.

    / 012 — MONEY YOU CAN AUDIT

    A ledger, not a balance.

    The client deposit is the money an endorsement draws on. We never store a number and update it; we store what happened. The balance is the sum of the entries, and each entry says who, why, and against which endorsement.

    • Append-onlyEntries are added, never edited. A correction is a new, opposite entry with its own reason.
    • Replay-safeRecording the same bank reference twice returns the original entry. A retried request cannot double-credit an account.
    • ExactAmounts with more than two decimals are refused, not quietly rounded. Reservations expire after 24 hours so a stuck job cannot lock funds.
    • Early warningA threshold per account raises a flag before an endorsement is blocked, and the dashboard shows which policies are low.

    / 013 — SECURITY BY CONSTRUCTION

    One door in. Everything else is closed.

    Customer data is the product, so the boundaries are structural rather than a checklist someone has to remember.

    • Gateway onlyEvery core service demands a shared secret and a per-session key, so a stolen token used directly against a service is refused.
    • Your tenantWho you are and which broker or employer you belong to come from the signed token, never from the request. Another tenant's record answers "not found".
    • No catch-allThe gateway has a written contract of exactly which calls exist. A path that is not in it does not exist.
    • Nothing leaksResponses are allow-lists, so a new internal field cannot reach the screen by accident, and errors never carry a stack trace.
    • Tokens stay homeThe portal keeps tokens in an encrypted server session. The browser never holds one.
    • WatchingRate limits per user and per tenant, decoy routes that log probes, and an audit trail on every state change.

    / 015 — UNDER THE HOOD

    Ten small services, each owning one thing.

    Each service has its own database and talks to the others through events and narrow APIs. The data a service does not own, it asks for or hears about, so one team's change cannot quietly break another's.
